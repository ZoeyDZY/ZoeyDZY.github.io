I am particularly interested in understanding how the **internal physics of neutron stars** can be probed through their **structure, dynamics, oscillations, and gravitational-wave signatures**.

## What might be inside a neutron star?

<div class="research-section">

  <div class="research-figure">

    <img

      src="/images/neutron-star-interior.png"

      alt="Schematic structure of a neutron star with a dense inner core"

    >

    <div class="research-caption">

      Schematic structure of a neutron star.

    </div>

  </div>

  <div class="research-text">

    <p>

      Matter is relatively well constrained around <strong>nuclear saturation density</strong>, while perturbative QCD provides guidance at asymptotically high densities. Neutron-star cores lie between these regimes, where the equation of state remains highly uncertain.

    </p>

    <p>

      The core may contain <strong>nucleonic matter, hyperons, deconfined quark matter, or other exotic phases</strong>, with possible phase transitions between them. Understanding the physics of matter in this regime is a central motivation for my research.

    </p>

  </div>

</div>

## How does the interior affect what we can observe?

<div class="research-section">

  <div class="research-figure">

    <img

      src="/images/neutron-star-tidal-deformation.png"

      alt="Schematic illustration of the tidal deformation of a neutron star in a binary system"

    >

    <div class="research-caption">

      Tidal deformation of a neutron star in a binary system.

    </div>

  </div>

  <div class="research-text">

    <p>

      A familiar example of tidal deformation is the <strong>Earth–Moon system</strong>: the Moon's gravitational field produces tides on Earth because the gravitational pull is not exactly the same across the Earth. The same basic idea applies to neutron stars in a binary system. As two neutron stars orbit each other, each star's gravitational field slightly deforms its companion.

    </p>

    <p>

      How easily a neutron star deforms depends on its <strong>internal structure and the properties of dense matter</strong>. This response, characterized by quantities such as the tidal deformability, can leave an imprint on the gravitational waves emitted during the inspiral. My previous work explored how exotic phases, including an elastic quark core, can modify these tidal properties.

    </p>

  </div>

</div>

## How can stellar dynamics reveal the interior?

<div class="research-section">

  <div class="research-figure">

    <img

      src="/images/neutron-star-oscillation.png"

      alt="Schematic illustration of a nonradially oscillating neutron star"

    >

    <div class="research-caption">

      Nonradial oscillations of a neutron star.

    </div>

  </div>

  <div class="research-text">

    <p>

      Like many physical systems, a neutron star can <strong>oscillate when it is perturbed</strong>. Different oscillation modes correspond to different patterns of motion inside the star, and their characteristic frequencies depend on the star's mass, radius, composition, and internal structure.

    </p>

    <p>

      Because an oscillating neutron star can produce a time-varying gravitational field, some of these oscillations can generate <strong>gravitational waves</strong>. Measuring their characteristic frequencies could therefore provide a form of <strong>asteroseismology</strong>: just as seismic waves reveal the Earth's interior, stellar oscillations can carry information about otherwise inaccessible regions inside a neutron star. My recent work explores how phase transitions and their dynamics may leave signatures in these oscillation modes.

    </p>

  </div>

</div>
